There is a version of this article that lists statistics.

It talks about how Black women are diagnosed with obesity at higher rates, how we are more likely to have our concerns dismissed in a clinical setting, how the weight loss industry has historically been built around a body type that was never ours.

But you already know the statistics. You have lived them.

What you may not know is why your body responds differently — and why that difference is not a defect.

Black women carry a particular kind of weight that has nothing to do with what we eat. We carry the weight of being the strong one. The caretaker. The one who holds everything together while quietly falling apart. Chronic stress releases cortisol, and cortisol tells your body to store fat — especially around the midsection — as a survival mechanism. Your body is not broken. It is responding rationally to an irrational amount of pressure.

We also carry the weight of medical mistrust — and rightfully so. Generations of being dismissed, misdiagnosed, undertreated, and made to feel like our pain was exaggerated have taught us to avoid doctors until something is seriously wrong. That means we often come to weight loss care later, with more metabolic ground to make up, and with less reason to trust the person sitting across from us.

That is exactly why representation in your healthcare provider matters.

At RefynMe, you will not be handed a meal plan that ignores how you actually eat or what food means in your family. You will not be told to just exercise more by someone who has never had to choose between a gym membership and a utility bill. You will be seen as a full person — with a full history — and treated accordingly.

Medical weight loss, including GLP-1 therapy, works. But it works best when it is delivered with cultural competence, real conversation, and genuine care.

That is what we offer. For you.
