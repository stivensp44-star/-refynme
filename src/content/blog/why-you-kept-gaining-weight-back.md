If you have lost weight before — and gained it back — you know the specific shame of that cycle.

You did everything right. You lost the weight. People noticed. You felt good. And then slowly, quietly, it came back. And this time it felt worse, because now you had proof that you could do it and still could not keep it.

What happened is not a character flaw. It is biology.

When you lose weight through calorie restriction alone, your body interprets it as a famine. It responds by lowering your metabolism, increasing hunger hormones, and decreasing satiety signals — all to drive you back to your previous weight. This is called metabolic adaptation, and it is one of the most well-documented phenomena in obesity medicine.

In other words: the harder you try through willpower alone, the harder your own body works against you.
